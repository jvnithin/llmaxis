## **AI Jobs in the USA: Skills Employers Actually Want**

Open a few AI Engineer job descriptions from US companies and you will notice something interesting.

One employer asks for Python, machine learning and PyTorch. Another wants experience with Large Language Models (LLMs), RAG and vector databases. A third is looking for someone who can build AI agents, integrate APIs and deploy applications on AWS.

The titles might look similar, but the technical expectations can be quite different.

This creates a common problem for job seekers: there are hundreds of AI tools, frameworks and technologies to learn, but which ones are actually appearing in employer requirements?

Learning every new AI framework is neither realistic nor necessary. What matters is understanding the capabilities employers are looking for and how those capabilities fit into real engineering work.

To answer that question, this article examines US job-posting data, emerging AI skill requirements and the technical capabilities that are becoming important across AI engineering, machine learning and Generative AI positions.

**The central finding:** Employers increasingly want professionals who can build, integrate, evaluate and deploy AI systems---not simply people who know how to use AI chatbots.

*Data note: This article uses the Stanford AI Index 2026, Lightcast\'s 2025 US job-posting analysis and a separate October 2026 snapshot of 1,288 US AI Engineer listings. Figures are identified by their source and measurement period.*

## **1. What do US employers actually look for in AI candidates?**

A useful starting point is the Stanford AI Index 2026, which uses job-posting data from Lightcast.

The report found that AI skills appeared in approximately 2.5% of all US job postings in 2025, representing a 55% increase compared with 2024.

But the more interesting finding is what employers were asking candidates to know.

According to Lightcast, Python was the most frequently requested specialised skill in US AI job postings, appearing in 258,674 postings in 2025.

Demand for Python in these postings had increased nearly 30% compared with 2024.

The report also identified growing demand for deployment-oriented capabilities, including AWS, scalability and workflow management.

This tells us something important about the market: AI engineering is increasingly about building systems that work reliably in production.

[[Source: Lightcast -- The Stanford AI Index Report 2026]{.underline}](https://lightcast.io/resources/research/stanford-ai-index-2026)

### **What is changing in employer expectations?**

Earlier AI job descriptions often placed considerable emphasis on traditional machine learning, statistical modelling and data science.

These skills remain relevant. However, employers developing modern AI applications increasingly need people who can:

- Work with foundation models and LLM APIs.

- Connect AI models to company data.

- Develop AI agents and automated workflows.

- Integrate models with existing applications.

- Evaluate output quality and reliability.

- Deploy applications to cloud infrastructure.

- Monitor and maintain production systems.

The shift is from demonstrating familiarity with AI technology to demonstrating the ability to implement it.

## **3. Python: The most important programming skill**

Python is one of the most widely used programming languages in artificial intelligence.

It is used for data processing, machine learning, model development, backend services, AI integrations and automation.

The Lightcast data shows Python appearing in 258,674 US AI job postings in 2025. The separate AI Engineer listing analysis found it in 69.2% of the descriptions examined.

These are different datasets, but both point in the same direction: Python remains central to AI engineering.

### **What do employers expect candidates to know?**

For AI engineering positions, Python knowledge typically extends beyond basic syntax.

Employers may expect familiarity with:

- Functions, classes and object-oriented programming.

- Data structures and algorithms.

- File handling and data processing.

- Exception handling and debugging.

- Asynchronous programming.

- Package management and virtual environments.

- Working with external APIs.

- Building backend applications.

Python libraries such as NumPy, Pandas, Pydantic and FastAPI are also useful in different parts of AI application development.

### **Why does this matter?**

Consider a company building an AI-powered customer support platform.

The engineer may need to receive a customer request, retrieve relevant information, call an LLM, validate the response and return it through a backend API.

Python can connect these components into a functioning application.

Knowing how to write prompts is useful. Knowing how to build the software around those prompts is a different capability.

## **4. Large Language Models (LLMs): A major requirement in Generative AI jobs**

Large Language Models are the foundation of many modern Generative AI applications.

Models from providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and other developers can be integrated into software products to support text generation, document analysis, reasoning and conversational interfaces.

In the October 2026 AI Engineer job-description analysis, LLMs appeared in 55.9% of the postings examined.

This makes LLM knowledge one of the most relevant capabilities for candidates interested in Generative AI engineering.

### **What should an AI engineer understand about LLMs?**

Employers may expect candidates to understand:

- How LLMs process and generate text.

- Tokens and context windows.

- Model selection and trade-offs.

- System and user prompts.

- Temperature and generation parameters.

- Structured outputs.

- Function and tool calling.

- API-based model integration.

- Latency and token costs.

- Model limitations and hallucinations.

A candidate should also understand that different models have different capabilities, pricing structures, latency characteristics and deployment options.

### **Why is this important?**

Imagine a financial services company building an application that analyses financial documents.

An engineer needs to select an appropriate model, process documents, send information through an API, obtain structured responses and validate the output.

That requires a combination of LLM knowledge, programming and application engineering.

## **5. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): Connecting AI to company knowledge**

An LLM can generate responses based on the information available to it, but organisations often need applications that answer questions using their own internal data.

That is where Retrieval-Augmented Generation, commonly called RAG, becomes useful.

RAG allows an application to retrieve relevant information from external knowledge sources and provide it to an LLM as context for generating a response.

In the October 2026 AI Engineer job-posting analysis, RAG appeared in 34.8% of the descriptions examined.

### **What does RAG involve?**

A typical RAG application may include:

1.  Document ingestion.

2.  Text extraction and processing.

3.  Chunking documents into smaller sections.

4.  Generating embeddings.

5.  Storing embeddings in a vector database.

6.  Retrieving relevant information for a user query.

7.  Passing retrieved context to an LLM.

8.  Generating and evaluating the response.

### **Technologies associated with RAG**

  -------------------------- -----------------------------------------
        **Technology**                    **Common use**

          Embeddings          Representing text as numerical vectors

            FAISS             Similarity search and vector retrieval

           Pinecone                   Managed vector database

            Chroma                 Vector storage and retrieval

   PostgreSQL with pgvector   Vector search alongside relational data

          LangChain           Framework for building LLM applications

          LlamaIndex          Data ingestion and retrieval workflows
  -------------------------- -----------------------------------------

A candidate does not need to know every vector database or framework. Understanding retrieval, relevance, document processing and evaluation is more important than memorising tool names.

### **Where do companies use RAG?**

Examples include:

- Internal company knowledge assistants.

- Legal document search.

- Financial research assistants.

- Customer support knowledge systems.

- Healthcare documentation search.

- Enterprise policy and compliance applications.

RAG is especially relevant when organisations need an AI application to work with frequently updated or proprietary information.

## **6. Machine Learning: The foundation still matters**

The growth of Generative AI has not made traditional machine learning irrelevant.

Machine learning remains important for recommendation systems, fraud detection, forecasting, classification, prediction and many other applications.

In the October 2026 AI Engineer job-posting analysis, machine learning appeared in 31.5% of the descriptions examined.

### **What should candidates understand?**

Depending on the role, employers may expect knowledge of:

- Supervised and unsupervised learning.

- Classification and regression.

- Feature engineering.

- Model training and validation.

- Overfitting and underfitting.

- Model evaluation metrics.

- Data preprocessing.

- Neural networks.

- Deep learning fundamentals.

Candidates working in more specialised machine learning positions may also need strong mathematics, statistics and experience with deep learning architectures.

### **Important distinction: AI Engineer vs. Machine Learning Engineer**

Although the job titles sometimes overlap, they can represent different types of work.

  ------------------------------------------------------ -------------------------------------------------------------
                     **AI Engineer**                                     **Machine Learning Engineer**
              Builds AI-powered applications                     Develops and deploys machine learning models
            Integrates LLMs and external APIs               Works extensively with training and inference pipelines
             Often works with RAG and agents              Often works with feature engineering and model optimisation
   Focuses on application functionality and integration     Focuses on model performance and production ML systems
            May use existing foundation models                      May train, fine-tune or optimise models
  ------------------------------------------------------ -------------------------------------------------------------

This is a general distinction, not a universal rule. Individual employers define these positions differently.

## **7. AI Agents and Agentic AI: An emerging hiring signal**

One of the most noticeable changes in AI job descriptions is the growing interest in AI agents.

Traditional chatbots primarily respond to user messages. AI agents can be designed to use tools, interact with systems and perform multi-step tasks toward a defined objective.

For example, an AI agent in a business environment might:

- Receive a customer request.

- Search internal documentation.

- Retrieve relevant account information.

- Call an external API.

- Prepare a response.

- Request human approval before executing a sensitive action.

The Stanford AI Index 2026 shows how quickly this area has entered employer job descriptions.

### **What should candidates understand about AI agents?**

Relevant concepts and technologies include:

- Tool calling and function calling.

- Agent planning and execution.

- Memory and state management.

- Workflow orchestration.

- Human-in-the-loop approvals.

- Multi-agent coordination.

- Error handling and recovery.

- Agent evaluation and monitoring.

Frameworks such as LangGraph, CrewAI and AutoGen are examples of tools used in agent development.

However, employers are not simply looking for candidates who have tried these frameworks. They need engineers who understand when an agent is appropriate, how to control its behaviour and how to make it reliable.

## **8. Cloud platforms: AWS, Azure and Google Cloud**

An AI application that works on a developer\'s laptop is not necessarily ready for business use.

Companies need AI systems that can be deployed, scaled, secured and maintained.

This is why cloud skills are relevant in AI engineering job descriptions.

In the October 2026 AI Engineer sample, AWS appeared in 26.9% of postings, GCP in 16.9% and Azure in 16.1%.

### **AWS**

Amazon Web Services is widely used for cloud infrastructure, data storage, application hosting and AI deployment.

Relevant services include:

- Amazon EC2.

- Amazon S3.

- AWS Lambda.

- Amazon Bedrock.

- Amazon SageMaker.

- Amazon ECS and EKS.

### **Microsoft Azure**

Azure is particularly relevant in organisations that use Microsoft\'s enterprise ecosystem.

Relevant services include:

- Azure Machine Learning.

- Azure AI Foundry.

- Azure OpenAI.

- Azure Functions.

- Azure Kubernetes Service.

- Azure Blob Storage.

### **Google Cloud Platform**

Google Cloud supports data analytics, machine learning and AI application development.

Relevant services include:

- Vertex AI.

- BigQuery.

- Cloud Run.

- Google Kubernetes Engine.

- Cloud Storage.

### **Do you need all three?**

No.

For most candidates, it is more useful to understand cloud fundamentals and gain practical experience with one platform before exploring others.

The ability to deploy an AI application, manage credentials, configure infrastructure and monitor usage is more valuable than listing three cloud providers on a résumé without practical experience.

## **9. APIs and backend development: Making AI useful in real applications**

AI systems rarely operate independently.

They need to communicate with databases, software platforms, payment systems, customer relationship management tools and other applications.

This is why API integration and backend development are important skills.

The October 2026 AI Engineer sample found REST APIs in 16.2% of the job descriptions examined.

### **What should candidates understand?**

- REST API fundamentals.

- HTTP methods and status codes.

- JSON data structures.

- Authentication and API keys.

- Request and response handling.

- Rate limits and retries.

- Error handling.

- Webhooks.

- Backend application development.

Frameworks such as FastAPI and Flask are commonly used to develop Python-based services.

### **A practical example**

Imagine an AI-powered sales assistant used by a US company.

It may need to:

1.  Receive a sales representative\'s question.

2.  Retrieve a customer\'s information from a CRM.

3.  Analyse previous interactions.

4.  Use an LLM to generate a recommendation.

5.  Return the recommendation to the sales application.

The AI model is only one part of the system. The APIs and backend services make the entire workflow possible.

## **10. PyTorch and deep learning frameworks**

For candidates targeting machine learning engineering and research-oriented roles, deep learning frameworks remain important.

PyTorch is widely used for developing and training neural networks.

TensorFlow is another established framework used in machine learning applications.

In the October 2026 AI Engineer job-posting sample, PyTorch appeared in 16.8% of descriptions.

### **Where is PyTorch useful?**

- Neural network development.

- Deep learning experiments.

- Model training and fine-tuning.

- Computer vision.

- Natural language processing.

- Model optimisation.

The depth required depends on the job.

A Generative AI application engineer integrating existing foundation models may not need the same PyTorch expertise as a research engineer developing or fine-tuning neural networks.

Candidates should read the job description carefully rather than assuming all AI positions require advanced model training.

## **11. MLOps, CI/CD and Kubernetes: Moving AI from development to production**

A successful AI prototype does not automatically become a reliable production application.

Companies need processes for deploying models, managing versions, monitoring performance and handling failures.

This is where MLOps and software delivery practices become important.

### **What is MLOps?**

MLOps combines machine learning development with software engineering and operational practices.

It helps organisations manage the lifecycle of machine learning systems, from development and experimentation through deployment and monitoring.

These skills matter because businesses want AI systems that continue working after deployment.

## **12. AI evaluation and observability: Can you prove that the AI system works?**

One of the most important engineering challenges in Generative AI is evaluating output quality.

Traditional software systems can often be tested against predictable inputs and expected outputs.

LLM applications are different. The same question can produce different responses, and a response may sound convincing while still being incorrect.

A production AI engineer must therefore think about reliability, not just functionality.

### **What is AI evaluation?**

AI evaluation involves measuring whether a model or AI application produces appropriate, accurate and useful outputs.

It may involve:

- Testing against representative datasets.

- Measuring retrieval relevance.

- Checking factual correctness.

- Evaluating hallucinations.

- Testing prompt variations.

- Measuring response latency.

- Comparing model performance.

- Reviewing outputs with human evaluators.

### **What is observability?**

Observability helps engineering teams understand what is happening inside a running application.

For AI systems, it can include monitoring:

- Model and API failures.

- Response latency.

- Token consumption.

- Retrieval performance.

- Error rates.

- Cost per request.

- Output quality.

- Agent tool-call behaviour.

The October 2026 job-posting sample included observability in 13.7% of descriptions.

These capabilities are especially important for applications that process customer information, financial documents or other business-critical data.

## **13. Do employers want AI skills beyond technical knowledge?**

Yes. Technical ability is essential for engineering positions, but it is not the only factor employers evaluate.

The Bipartisan Policy Center\'s September 2026 analysis of Lightcast job-posting data highlighted continued employer demand for non-AI capabilities alongside AI skills.

These include:

- Communication.

- Problem-solving.

- Management.

- Leadership.

- Workflow management.

McKinsey Global Institute\'s research also shows how demand for AI fluency is spreading beyond specialist technical occupations.

Its analysis of US job postings found that the number of workers in occupations where AI fluency was requested in at least 5% of postings increased from approximately one million in 2023 to seven million in 2025.

This is a different measure from technical AI engineering demand. It reflects the growing importance of using, managing and working alongside AI, not only building AI systems.

### **Why do these skills matter?**

An AI engineer may be technically capable of building a RAG application, but the business still needs someone who can understand the problem, communicate with stakeholders and explain the trade-offs.

For example, a healthcare company may need an AI application that reduces administrative work without compromising privacy or accuracy.

The engineer must understand the business requirement, communicate technical limitations and work with other teams to deliver a reliable solution.

Technical knowledge gets the application built. Problem-solving and communication help make it useful.

## **14. Are AI Engineer jobs in the USA mostly senior positions?**

This is an important question, especially for recent graduates and professionals trying to enter the field.

The October 2026 Apiva analysis identified 1,288 live US AI Engineer listings.

Only 4.9% were classified as open to entry-level candidates, representing 63 internship or junior postings.

The report also found that, among analysed postings that stated a numerical experience requirement, half requested five or more years of experience.

This suggests that dedicated AI Engineer positions can be relatively senior-heavy.

However, the data should be interpreted carefully:

- The entry-level classification depends on the provider\'s methodology.

- Some companies use different job titles for early-career opportunities.

- Internship, graduate software engineering and machine learning positions may not be counted as AI Engineer roles.

- The 715 descriptions analysed were a selected subset, not a random sample of all postings.

[[Source: Apiva -- October 2026 AI Engineer Job Market Report]{.underline}](https://kitejobs.ai/reports/ai-engineer)

### **What does this mean for fresh graduates?**

A graduate should not restrict their search to positions titled \"AI Engineer.\"

Related opportunities may include:

- Junior Software Engineer.

- Associate Machine Learning Engineer.

- Data Engineer.

- AI Application Developer.

- Research Assistant.

- Software Engineer -- AI/ML.

- AI-focused internships.

Some candidates enter AI through conventional software engineering or data roles and later move into more specialised AI positions.

## **15. What salary do US employers offer for AI engineering skills?**

Compensation varies according to the position, location, employer, experience and technical specialisation.

### **Does knowing more AI tools automatically mean a higher salary?**

Not necessarily.

Employers pay for the value and complexity of the work a candidate can perform.

A professional who can design a reliable AI platform, optimise inference costs, manage infrastructure and solve production problems may command a different compensation package from someone whose experience is limited to building simple LLM demonstrations.

Specialisation, experience, business impact and the level of responsibility all matter.

## **16. What should candidates prioritise when evaluating AI job requirements?**

The data suggests that employers are looking for a combination of programming, AI application development and production engineering.

It is useful to group the capabilities into four categories.

  ------------------------ ------------------------------------------------------ -------------------------------------------------
    **Capability area**                     **Important skills**                            **Why employers value them**
    Software foundations               Python, APIs, TypeScript, SQL                Building applications and integrating systems
       AI development             LLMs, machine learning, RAG, embeddings                Developing intelligent applications
      Agentic systems           Tool calling, LangGraph, agent orchestration       Building systems that perform multi-step tasks
   Production engineering   AWS, Azure, Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD, observability   Deploying and maintaining reliable applications
  ------------------------ ------------------------------------------------------ -------------------------------------------------

The most important point is that these are complementary capabilities.

Knowing Python without understanding how to integrate an AI model may limit the type of work a candidate can perform.

Likewise, understanding LLMs without knowing how to deploy and monitor an application may not satisfy employers looking for production engineering experience.

The strongest candidates can connect these areas.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **1. What is the most important skill for AI jobs in the USA?**

Python is one of the most frequently requested skills. In Lightcast\'s 2025 analysis, it appeared in 258,674 US AI job postings. A separate October 2026 AI Engineer sample found Python mentioned in 69.2% of analysed job descriptions.

### **2. Do AI engineers need to know Python?**

Python is highly relevant to most AI engineering and machine learning positions. It is widely used for data processing, model integration, backend development and AI application development. The exact programming requirements depend on the role.

### **3. Are LLMs required for AI engineering jobs?**

LLM knowledge is particularly relevant for Generative AI and applied AI positions. In the October 2026 AI Engineer job-description analysis, LLMs appeared in 55.9% of the descriptions examined.

### **4. Is RAG a required skill for AI engineers?**

RAG is increasingly relevant to engineers building applications that need to retrieve information from company documents, databases and external knowledge sources. It appeared in 34.8% of the analysed AI Engineer job descriptions in the October 2026 sample. It is not mandatory for every AI position.

### **5. Do AI engineers need to learn LangChain and LangGraph?**

These frameworks can be useful for developing LLM applications and agent workflows. However, employers are more interested in whether candidates understand the underlying concepts, such as retrieval, orchestration, tool calling, state management and evaluation.

### **6. Is machine learning still necessary after the growth of Generative AI?**

Yes. Machine learning remains important in forecasting, recommendation systems, fraud detection, computer vision, model development and many other applications. The level of machine learning knowledge required depends on the job.

### **7. Do AI engineers need cloud computing skills?**

Cloud knowledge is valuable because companies need to deploy and maintain AI applications. Familiarity with at least one major cloud platform, such as AWS, Azure or Google Cloud, can be useful.

### **8. Do fresh graduates have a chance of getting AI jobs in the USA?**

Yes, but dedicated entry-level AI Engineer openings can be limited. Candidates should also consider internships, graduate software engineering positions, data engineering and machine learning roles. Practical technical experience can help demonstrate readiness.

### **9. Which AI skills are growing fastest in US job postings?**

Lightcast\'s 2025 data shows particularly rapid growth in Agentic AI, AI agents and LangGraph mentions. Agentic AI increased from 151 postings in 2024 to 16,541 in 2025. This is a skill-mention measure, not a count of dedicated Agentic AI Engineer vacancies.

### **10. Are soft skills important for AI engineers?**

Yes. Communication, analytical thinking, problem-solving and the ability to understand business requirements help engineers work effectively with product teams, customers and other technical professionals.

## **Conclusion: Employers Want Engineers Who Can Build Real AI Systems**

The US AI job market is becoming more demanding, but the direction of employer expectations is becoming clearer.

Python remains a major foundation. LLMs and RAG are prominent in Generative AI engineering. AI agents are becoming a visible hiring signal. Cloud infrastructure, APIs, CI/CD and observability matter because companies need AI applications that work reliably outside a development environment.

The difference between a candidate who knows AI terminology and one who can contribute to an engineering team often comes down to practical implementation.

Employers want professionals who can understand a business problem, select the right technology, build an application, evaluate its performance and deploy it reliably.

That is a more meaningful way to think about AI engineering skills than simply collecting certificates or learning every new framework.

**The most valuable AI engineers will not necessarily be the ones who know the most tools. They will be the ones who know how to use the right tools to solve real problems.**
