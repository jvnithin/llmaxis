## **Can AI Students Qualify for STEM OPT in the USA?**

Yes. International students studying Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the United States may qualify for the 24-month STEM OPT extension if their degree program meets the Department of Homeland Security\'s STEM requirements and they satisfy the applicable F-1 student, employment, and application conditions.

However, **studying AI does not automatically make a student eligible for STEM OPT**.

The degree\'s official Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) code, the institution\'s eligibility, the student\'s current OPT status, and the nature of the employment all matter. A program called *Master of Artificial Intelligence* may qualify, while another program with a similar title may not. Students need to verify the actual degree and CIP code rather than relying on the program name.

For international students planning an AI career in the USA, understanding this distinction before choosing a university or degree program can help avoid expensive surprises later.

This guide explains how STEM OPT works for AI students, which degree types may qualify, how to check a CIP code, what employers must provide, and how to prepare for the application process.

> **Important:** This article provides general educational information, not legal or immigration advice. STEM OPT eligibility and application decisions depend on individual circumstances and current government rules. Confirm your situation with your Designated School Official (DSO) and, when appropriate, a qualified immigration attorney.

## **1. What Is STEM OPT?**

Optional Practical Training (OPT) is a form of temporary employment authorization available to eligible F-1 international students. It allows students to gain practical experience related to their major field of study.

Most eligible F-1 students can apply for up to 12 months of post-completion OPT after completing an academic program.

Students who meet additional requirements may apply for a **24-month STEM OPT extension**. This can provide up to 36 months of post-completion OPT in total: the initial 12 months plus the additional 24 months.

  --------------------- -------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------
     **Type of OPT**                          **Duration**                                                 **Purpose**
   Pre-completion OPT    Available before program completion, subject to limits                 Practical training during studies
   Post-completion OPT                 Generally up to 12 months                           Practical training after completing a degree
   STEM OPT extension                     Additional 24 months                    Extended practical training for eligible STEM degree graduates
  --------------------- -------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------

The STEM extension is not a separate work visa or an automatic benefit of graduating from a technology program. Students must meet the requirements and obtain the necessary authorization.

Official resources: [[USCIS -- STEM OPT]{.underline}](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/students-and-employment/stem-opt) and [[DHS -- STEM OPT Hub]{.underline}](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub).

## **2. Do AI and Computer Science Degrees Qualify for STEM OPT?**

They may. Many AI-related academic programs are built around subjects such as computer science, mathematics, statistics, engineering, and data science. These fields can fall within DHS-designated STEM areas.

But there is no safe rule that every degree with "AI," "machine learning," or "data science" in its title qualifies.

The key is the degree\'s **CIP code** and whether that code appears on the current DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List.

Examples of degree areas students may want to investigate include:

     **Degree or field**     **STEM OPT possibility**                   **What to verify**
   Artificial Intelligence         May qualify                      Exact CIP code on the I-20
      Computer Science             May qualify                    Program\'s designated CIP code
      Machine Learning             May qualify          Whether the program\'s CIP code is on the DHS list
        Data Science               May qualify                     Exact degree classification
    Computer Engineering           May qualify                   Program CIP code and institution
     Applied Mathematics           May qualify                            Exact CIP code
   Information Technology     Depends on the program          Do not assume all IT programs qualify
     Business Analytics       Depends on the program        Verify the official degree classification
  ------------------------- -------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------

These are examples of areas to investigate, not a list of guaranteed eligible degrees.

### **Why the degree title is not enough**

Universities can use different names for programs with similar course content. The immigration classification attached to the program is what matters for STEM OPT purposes.

For example:

- University A offers an MS in Artificial Intelligence.

- University B offers an MS in Applied Data Analytics.

- University C offers an MS in Computer Science with an AI concentration.

All three may contain AI-related subjects. That does not mean they automatically have the same STEM OPT eligibility.

A student should verify the actual CIP code assigned to the specific degree program.

## **3. What Is a CIP Code, and Why Does It Matter?**

CIP stands for **Classification of Instructional Programs**. It is a system developed by the U.S. Department of Education to classify academic programs and fields of study.

For STEM OPT purposes, DHS uses designated CIP codes to identify qualifying STEM degree fields.

Your CIP code is generally shown in the Program of Study section on page one of your Form I-20.

### **How to check whether your AI degree qualifies**

Follow these steps:

1.  **Find your CIP code:** Check the Program of Study section of your Form I-20.

2.  **Open the DHS STEM list:** Visit the official [[ICE STEM Designated Degree Program List]{.underline}](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/schools).

3.  **Search for the exact code:** Compare the code on your I-20 with the current designated list.

4.  **Confirm with your DSO:** Ask your university\'s international student office to verify the program\'s classification and your eligibility.

5.  **Check the employment connection:** Your practical training must be directly related to the qualifying STEM degree.

Do not rely only on a university marketing page, a course title, or a general statement that a program is STEM-related.

### **Questions to ask a university before enrolling**

If you are comparing AI master\'s programs in the USA, ask the admissions or international student office:

- What CIP code is assigned to this exact degree program?

- Is this CIP code currently on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List?

- Will the CIP code appear on my Form I-20?

- Has the university\'s international student office confirmed the program\'s STEM OPT classification?

- If the program has concentrations or specializations, does the CIP code change depending on the chosen track?

Get clear answers before making your enrollment decision.

## **4. What Are the Main STEM OPT Eligibility Requirements for AI Students?**

A qualifying AI-related degree is only one part of the process. Students must also satisfy the applicable F-1 status, OPT, employment, and application requirements.

The main requirements include:

            **Requirement**                                             **What it means**
        Qualifying STEM degree                     The degree must meet the DHS STEM designation requirements
         Eligible institution          The qualifying degree must meet applicable U.S. accreditation and SEVP requirements
              Current OPT                         The student must be in a valid period of post-completion OPT
   Direct relationship to the degree      The practical training must be directly related to the qualifying STEM degree

A student\'s university international office should confirm how these requirements apply to their individual case.

### **Does the student need to be employed?**

STEM OPT is based on a qualifying practical-training opportunity. Students generally need a qualifying paid position with an employer that meets the STEM OPT requirements.

The position must provide practical training directly related to the student\'s qualifying STEM degree. A job title alone does not establish that relationship.

For example, an AI graduate working on machine learning model development may be able to demonstrate a direct connection to a qualifying AI or computer science degree. A position that is primarily unrelated to the degree may not satisfy the requirement simply because the employer is a technology company.

## **5. What Employers Must Provide for STEM OPT**

The employer plays an important role in the STEM OPT process. A company offering an AI or machine learning position must be able to meet the relevant regulatory requirements and participate in the student\'s training plan.

### **E-Verify enrollment**

The employer must be enrolled in E-Verify, the federal employment eligibility verification system.

Students should ask potential employers whether the specific hiring entity is enrolled. Do not assume that every company, subsidiary, staffing firm, or client site connected to a large technology brand automatically meets the requirement.

### **Form I-983 training plan**

The student and employer must complete Form I-983, the Training Plan for STEM OPT Students.

The form documents how the job provides structured, degree-related practical training. It covers areas such as:

- The student\'s learning objectives.

- The knowledge and skills the student will develop.

- How the employer will provide supervision and training.

- How progress and performance will be evaluated.

- How the work relates to the student\'s qualifying STEM degree.

The employer must have an appropriate person complete and sign the relevant sections.

### **A genuine employer-employee relationship**

The training arrangement must meet the applicable employer-employee relationship requirements. The employer must have the ability to provide and oversee the training described in the plan.

A job offer from a company that cannot provide the required supervision or training is not automatically a qualifying STEM OPT opportunity.

### **Work hours and compensation**

STEM OPT employment generally must be paid and meet the minimum hours requirement. The regulations require at least 20 hours per week for each qualifying employer.

Students considering contract, consulting, staffing, or remote arrangements should ask their DSO to review the structure carefully. The arrangement must satisfy the applicable requirements, not merely provide a job title or a project assignment.

More information: [[DHS -- Employers and Form I-983]{.underline}](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/employers-and-the-form-i-983).

## **7. Can an AI Certificate or Bootcamp Make You Eligible for STEM OPT?**

No. Completing an AI certificate, online course, bootcamp, or short-term professional training program does not independently qualify someone for the 24-month STEM OPT extension.

STEM OPT is tied to qualifying academic degrees and the applicable F-1 practical-training framework.

AI courses and certifications can still be useful for developing practical skills, building projects, and preparing for technical interviews. They simply do not replace the qualifying degree or immigration requirements.

For students who already have F-1 status, it is important to distinguish between **career preparation** and **employment authorization**.

## **8. Can Students in Non-AI Majors Get STEM OPT for an AI Job?**

Possibly, but the degree and job relationship must be evaluated carefully.

A student does not necessarily need a degree with the words "Artificial Intelligence" in its title to pursue an AI career or qualify for STEM OPT.

For example:

- A Computer Science graduate may pursue a machine learning engineering role.

- A Mathematics graduate may work on statistical modelling or machine learning.

- A Data Science graduate may pursue data science or AI engineering work.

- An Electrical or Computer Engineering graduate may work in computer vision or AI hardware.

The important question is whether the qualifying degree meets the STEM OPT requirements and whether the practical training is directly related to that degree.

### **What if your degree is in business analytics or information systems?**

Do not assume that every business analytics, management information systems, or information technology program qualifies.

Some programs may have STEM-designated CIP codes, while others may not. The precise classification on the I-20 is what students should investigate.

If you are planning to move from a nontechnical background into AI, compare the curriculum, degree classification, and career outcomes before selecting a program.

## **9. Can You Use a Previous STEM Degree to Apply for STEM OPT?**

In certain circumstances, yes.

A student currently on post-completion OPT based on a more recent non-STEM degree may be able to use a previously earned qualifying STEM degree from an eligible U.S. institution.

For example:

1.  A student completes a qualifying STEM bachelor\'s degree in Computer Science at a U.S. institution.

2.  The student later completes a non-STEM master\'s degree.

3.  The student receives post-completion OPT based on the more recent master\'s degree.

4.  Under the applicable rules, the student may be able to seek a STEM OPT extension based on the earlier Computer Science degree.

This is subject to specific conditions. These include the prior degree\'s eligibility, the institution and accreditation requirements, the timing of the degree, the student\'s current OPT status, and whether the practical training is directly related to the earlier STEM degree.

This is not an automatic route for every student with a previous technical qualification. A degree earned outside the United States does not automatically serve as the qualifying prior degree for this purpose.

Read the official guidance and ask your DSO to evaluate your circumstances: [[USCIS Policy Manual -- Practical Training]{.underline}](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-f/chapter-5).

## **10. When Should AI Students Apply for STEM OPT?**

Timing matters. Students should begin discussing the process with their university\'s international student office well before their initial post-completion OPT expires.

The general application sequence is:

1.  Confirm that the qualifying degree and CIP code meet the STEM requirements.

2.  Secure a qualifying employment opportunity with an eligible employer.

3.  Work with the employer to complete Form I-983.

4.  Submit the required documents to the DSO.

5.  Obtain an updated Form I-20 with the STEM OPT recommendation.

6.  File Form I-765 with USCIS within the permitted application window.

7.  Follow USCIS instructions and monitor the application.

### **Important application timing**

Under the current STEM OPT process, a student generally may file the application up to 90 days before the current post-completion OPT EAD expires. The DSO\'s STEM OPT recommendation must also be recent: USCIS must receive the application within 60 days of that recommendation.

Students should not wait until the final days of their current work authorization to start preparing.

If a timely STEM OPT extension application is properly filed, the regulations provide for an automatic extension of employment authorization for up to 180 days while the application is pending, subject to the applicable conditions.

Always verify the current filing instructions and deadlines with your DSO and USCIS before submitting an application.

Official application resource: [[USCIS -- STEM OPT Extension]{.underline}](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/students-and-employment/stem-opt).

Students should select skills based on the roles they want to pursue rather than trying to learn every AI technology at once.

### **Build a portfolio with real projects**

A portfolio can help demonstrate practical ability to employers.

Useful project examples include:

**Project 1: RAG-based AI assistant**

Build an application that answers questions using a collection of documents. Include document ingestion, embeddings, retrieval, response generation, and evaluation.

**Project 2: AI-powered data analysis system**

Create a system that processes structured datasets, identifies trends, generates insights, and presents results through a dashboard or natural-language interface.

**Project 3: Agentic AI workflow**

Build an AI agent that can use tools, execute a sequence of tasks, handle errors, and produce a final result with traceable steps.

**Project 4: Machine learning deployment**

Train and evaluate a predictive model, expose it through an API, and deploy it using a cloud platform.

For each project, explain the problem, architecture, tools, technical decisions, evaluation approach, and results.

### **Gain relevant experience**

Students should explore internships, research opportunities, university projects, and other permitted practical experiences.

Remember that an internship or job during studies may require separate authorization, such as Curricular Practical Training (CPT). Do not assume that enrolling in a program or receiving an offer permits you to start working.

If you are an international student considering a master\'s degree in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Data Science, or Computer Science, use this checklist before accepting an offer.

- The university is appropriately accredited.

- The institution is authorized to enroll F-1 international students.

- I have the exact degree title and program details.

- I have asked for the program\'s CIP code.

- I have checked the CIP code against the current DHS STEM list.

- The university\'s international student office has confirmed the program\'s classification.

## **15. Frequently Asked Questions About STEM OPT for AI Students**

### **1. Can students pursuing a Master\'s in Artificial Intelligence get STEM OPT?**

Yes, potentially. The exact program must have a qualifying CIP code on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List, and the student must meet all other STEM OPT requirements.

### **2. Is a Master\'s in Data Science eligible for STEM OPT?**

Some Data Science programs may qualify. Students should verify the specific CIP code listed on their Form I-20 rather than relying on the degree title.

### **3. Does a Master\'s in Computer Science with an AI specialization qualify?**

It may. The official classification of the degree program matters. Ask the university\'s international student office to confirm the CIP code and STEM designation.

### **4. Can I get STEM OPT after completing an online AI certification?**

No. An online AI certification by itself does not provide eligibility for the STEM OPT extension. STEM OPT is linked to a qualifying academic degree and the applicable F-1 practical-training requirements.

### **5. Can I work as a Generative AI Engineer during STEM OPT?**

Potentially, if the job is directly related to your qualifying STEM degree and the employer and training arrangement meet all applicable requirements. The job title alone does not establish eligibility.

### **6. Does my employer need to be enrolled in E-Verify?**

Yes. The employer must meet the applicable STEM OPT employer requirements, including E-Verify enrollment.

### **7. Is Form I-983 compulsory for STEM OPT?**

Yes. The student and employer must complete the required training plan and submit it through the appropriate process to the student\'s DSO.

### **8. Can I get STEM OPT if my degree is not called Artificial Intelligence?**

Possibly. Degrees such as Computer Science, Mathematics, Engineering, or Data Science may qualify if the specific degree\'s CIP code is designated and all other requirements are satisfied.

### **9. Can I use my previous Computer Science degree for STEM OPT after completing a non-STEM master\'s?**

In certain circumstances, yes. A previously earned qualifying U.S. STEM degree may be used under specific conditions. The student must satisfy the applicable prior-degree rules and have practical training directly related to that qualifying degree.

### **10. Does STEM OPT guarantee an H-1B visa?**

No. STEM OPT is a temporary F-1 practical-training extension. It does not guarantee H-1B sponsorship, selection in the H-1B process, or permanent residence.

### **11. How long can I work in the USA through OPT and STEM OPT?**

Eligible students may generally have up to 12 months of post-completion OPT followed by an additional 24 months of STEM OPT, for up to 36 months in total. The student must meet the relevant requirements and obtain authorization.

### **12. Should I choose an AI master\'s program based on STEM OPT eligibility alone?**

No. STEM OPT eligibility is an important consideration, but students should also evaluate curriculum quality, faculty, projects, tuition, location, career services, employer connections, and the program\'s fit with their long-term career goals.
