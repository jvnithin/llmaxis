# **AI Jobs for Finance Professionals in 2027: Roles, Salaries, Skills and Career Opportunities in the USA**

Artificial intelligence is changing how financial institutions analyze information, manage risks, detect fraud and make business decisions.

Banks are using AI to identify suspicious transactions. Investment firms are exploring AI-powered research and analysis. Accounting teams are automating repetitive financial processes, while corporate finance departments are using AI to improve forecasting and reporting.

These changes are creating new opportunities for professionals who understand finance and can work with artificial intelligence.

But here is something worth understanding: **You do not necessarily need to become a software engineer to build a career in AI within the finance industry.**

A financial analyst who understands AI-powered financial modeling, an accountant who can automate reporting workflows, or a banking professional who understands AI-based fraud detection may be able to move into new technology-enabled roles by combining existing industry knowledge with relevant AI skills.

The opportunity is not limited to people with computer science degrees. Finance professionals already understand financial statements, compliance, transactions, business performance and risk. These capabilities can become valuable when organizations introduce AI into their financial operations.

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), financial analysts are projected to see 7% employment growth between 2025 and 2035, while accountants and auditors are projected to grow by 5% during the same period.

At the same time, AI adoption is changing the skills employers expect from finance professionals.

So, what AI jobs can finance professionals pursue in the United States in 2027? Which skills should they learn, and how can someone transition from a traditional finance role into an AI-focused career?

Let\'s explore the opportunities.

## **How Is AI Changing the Finance Industry?**

AI adoption in finance is moving beyond experimentation. Financial institutions are exploring applications across accounting, banking, investment management, insurance, compliance and financial planning.

Some examples include:

- **Financial analysis:** AI can help process financial reports, identify trends and summarize company performance.

- **Fraud detection:** Machine learning systems can analyze transaction patterns and flag potentially suspicious activity.

- **Accounting automation:** AI-powered tools can assist with invoice processing, reconciliation and financial documentation.

- **Risk management:** AI can help analyze financial data, identify risk patterns and support credit assessments.

- **Investment research:** AI tools can summarize market information, review company filings and support investment research.

- **Financial forecasting:** Predictive models can help businesses estimate revenue, expenses and cash flow.

The scale of adoption is becoming clearer.

A 2025 Gartner survey of 183 CFOs and senior finance leaders found that 59% of finance functions were using AI in 2025. This was up slightly from 58% in 2024, following a larger increase from 37% in 2023.

Separately, data from the Real-Time Population Survey, published through the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED database, shows that 63.9% of employed adults in the US finance and insurance industry reported using generative AI for work in July 2026.

These measurements use different methodologies and should not be compared directly. However, both indicate that AI is becoming part of everyday financial work.

For finance professionals, the important question is no longer simply whether AI will be used in their industry. It is how they can develop the skills to work effectively with these systems.

## **1. AI Financial Analyst**

An AI Financial Analyst uses artificial intelligence and financial analysis techniques to examine financial information, identify patterns and support business decisions.

Traditional financial analysts already work with income statements, balance sheets, cash flow reports, market data and financial models. AI adds another layer by helping them process information faster and explore patterns across larger datasets.

For example, imagine an investment firm evaluating several publicly traded companies. An AI-enabled financial analyst could use AI tools to summarize annual reports, extract financial metrics, compare company performance and identify changes in business trends.

The analyst still needs to validate the information, understand the financial context and make informed recommendations.

**What the role involves**

- Analyzing financial statements and business performance.

- Using AI tools to summarize financial reports and company filings.

- Supporting financial forecasting and valuation.

- Identifying trends in revenue, expenses and profitability.

- Preparing financial reports and management presentations.

- Validating AI-generated financial insights.

**Skills employers may look for**

Financial analysis, Excel, financial modeling, accounting fundamentals, SQL, Power BI, Python fundamentals, generative AI tools and data interpretation.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Financial analysts, investment analysts, FP&A professionals, accountants and finance graduates with strong analytical foundations.

**Salary context**

The BLS reported a median annual wage of \$103,570 for financial analysts in May 2025. This is a benchmark for the broader financial analyst occupation, not a separate salary figure for AI Financial Analysts.

## **2. AI Finance Automation Specialist**

Finance departments handle many repetitive processes, including invoice processing, expense management, reconciliation, reporting and financial documentation.

An AI Finance Automation Specialist identifies these processes and uses AI tools, automation platforms and workflow systems to reduce manual work.

For example, a company may receive hundreds of invoices every month. A finance automation specialist could help implement a workflow that extracts invoice information, categorizes expenses, checks records against purchase orders and sends exceptions to the appropriate employee for review.

The goal is not to remove financial controls. It is to make routine processes faster while maintaining accuracy and accountability.

**What the role involves**

- Identifying repetitive finance processes suitable for automation.

- Building automated invoice and expense workflows.

- Using AI to extract information from financial documents.

- Automating financial reporting and reconciliation tasks.

- Connecting finance applications through APIs and automation tools.

- Monitoring workflow accuracy and handling exceptions.

**Skills employers may look for**

Excel, Power Automate, UiPath, Zapier, Make, Python fundamentals, SQL, API fundamentals, document processing and generative AI tools.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Accountants, accounts payable and receivable professionals, finance operations specialists, payroll professionals and financial controllers.

Professionals with finance experience can begin by learning no-code and low-code automation before progressing to more technical AI integrations.

## **3. AI Risk Analyst**

Financial institutions must evaluate risks associated with lending, investments, market movements, fraud and financial operations.

An AI Risk Analyst works with data and AI-powered analytical systems to identify potential risks and support risk management decisions.

For example, a bank evaluating loan applications may use machine learning models to analyze applicant information and estimate the likelihood of repayment difficulties.

An AI Risk Analyst may help evaluate the model\'s results, investigate unusual patterns, identify potential bias and ensure that risk assessments are properly documented.

The role combines financial risk knowledge with data analysis and an understanding of how AI systems operate.

**What the role involves**

- Analyzing financial and operational risk data.

- Supporting AI-assisted credit risk assessments.

- Identifying unusual patterns and potential anomalies.

- Evaluating the reliability of AI-generated risk predictions.

- Monitoring model performance and reporting issues.

- Supporting risk documentation and governance processes.

**Skills employers may look for**

Financial risk management, statistics, Excel, SQL, Python, Power BI, machine learning fundamentals, model evaluation and data visualization.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Credit analysts, risk analysts, banking professionals, financial examiners, insurance analysts and professionals working in compliance or risk management.

**Salary context**

The BLS reported a median annual wage of \$117,330 for financial risk specialists in May 2025. This is a relevant occupational benchmark, not a dedicated salary estimate for AI Risk Analysts.

## **4. AI Fraud Detection Analyst**

Fraud detection is one of the important applications of AI in banking and financial services.

Financial institutions process enormous volumes of transactions. Identifying suspicious activity manually can be difficult, especially when fraudulent behavior changes over time.

AI-powered systems can analyze transaction patterns, customer behavior and historical data to identify activity that may require investigation.

An AI Fraud Detection Analyst works with these systems to investigate alerts, interpret suspicious patterns and support fraud prevention.

For example, if a customer\'s account suddenly shows unusual transaction activity across multiple locations, an AI system may flag the transactions for further review.

The analyst investigates the alert, examines the available evidence and determines the appropriate next steps under the organization\'s procedures.

**What the role involves**

- Investigating suspicious financial transactions.

- Reviewing alerts generated by AI fraud detection systems.

- Identifying unusual customer and transaction patterns.

- Supporting fraud prevention and detection strategies.

- Analyzing historical fraud data.

- Documenting investigation findings and recommendations.

**Skills employers may look for**

Fraud investigation, banking operations, transaction monitoring, Excel, SQL, data analysis, machine learning fundamentals and financial compliance.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Banking professionals, fraud investigators, transaction monitoring analysts, compliance professionals and financial operations specialists.

A background in banking or fraud investigation can provide a useful starting point. Learning how AI-based detection systems work can help professionals expand their capabilities.

## **5. AI Compliance and Governance Analyst**

Financial institutions operate under extensive regulatory and compliance requirements. As organizations introduce AI into financial operations, they also need to understand how these systems handle sensitive information, produce decisions and create potential risks.

An AI Compliance and Governance Analyst helps organizations evaluate whether AI applications follow internal policies, regulatory expectations and responsible AI practices.

For example, a financial institution using an AI-powered customer service assistant needs to consider data privacy, access permissions, information accuracy and how the system responds to sensitive financial questions.

An AI governance professional may help document these risks, review system behavior and coordinate with compliance, legal, risk and technology teams.

**What the role involves**

- Reviewing AI applications for compliance risks.

- Supporting AI governance policies and procedures.

- Monitoring data privacy and information security requirements.

- Documenting AI system risks and limitations.

- Supporting model risk management activities.

- Coordinating with legal, compliance and technology teams.

**Skills employers may look for**

Financial regulations, compliance, risk management, data privacy, AI fundamentals, documentation, analytical thinking and responsible AI principles.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Compliance officers, internal auditors, financial examiners, risk professionals, legal operations professionals and banking specialists.

This can be a relevant direction for professionals who understand financial regulations but do not want to move into software development.

## **6. AI Investment Research Analyst**

Investment research involves studying companies, industries, financial markets and economic developments to support investment decisions.

AI Investment Research Analysts use AI tools to process information, summarize company reports, compare financial performance and support investment research.

For example, an analyst researching a company may use AI to review its annual reports, extract important financial information, summarize management discussions and compare its performance with competitors.

However, AI-generated analysis must be checked against original documents and reliable financial information. Investment recommendations still require professional judgment and appropriate oversight.

**What the role involves**

- Reviewing financial statements and company filings.

- Using AI tools to summarize research documents.

- Comparing company and industry performance.

- Supporting valuation and investment analysis.

- Monitoring market developments.

- Preparing investment research reports.

**Skills employers may look for**

Financial modeling, equity research, valuation, accounting, Excel, market research, Python or SQL, generative AI tools and analytical reasoning.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Equity research analysts, investment banking professionals, portfolio analysts, financial analysts and professionals with experience in capital markets.

## **7. AI Financial Planning and Analysis (FP&A) Analyst**

Financial Planning and Analysis professionals help businesses understand their financial performance and plan for the future.

Their work includes budgeting, forecasting, expense analysis, financial reporting and supporting management decisions.

AI can assist FP&A teams by analyzing historical financial data, identifying spending patterns and generating initial forecasts.

For example, an AI-enabled FP&A analyst may examine several years of revenue and expense data, identify seasonal trends and prepare different financial scenarios for management to review.

The analyst remains responsible for interpreting the results and understanding the assumptions behind the forecasts.

**What the role involves**

- Preparing budgets and financial forecasts.

- Analyzing revenue, expenses and cash flow.

- Using AI tools to identify financial trends.

- Supporting scenario and sensitivity analysis.

- Automating recurring management reports.

- Presenting financial insights to business leaders.

**Skills employers may look for**

FP&A, budgeting, financial modeling, Excel, Power BI, SQL, financial forecasting, data analysis and generative AI tools.

**Who can pursue this role?**

FP&A analysts, management accountants, corporate finance professionals, financial controllers and business analysts.

## **8. AI Banking Operations Analyst**

Banks manage a wide range of operational activities, from customer onboarding and loan processing to payment operations and account servicing.

AI Banking Operations Analysts work with AI-powered systems to improve these processes, analyze operational data and support better service delivery.

For example, an AI-assisted loan processing workflow may extract information from submitted documents, identify missing records and route applications to the appropriate team.

An operations analyst can help monitor the process, investigate errors and identify opportunities to improve efficiency.

**What the role involves**

- Analyzing banking operations and service processes.

- Supporting AI-enabled loan and document processing.

- Improving customer onboarding workflows.

- Identifying operational bottlenecks.

- Monitoring automation accuracy and exceptions.

- Coordinating with technology and banking teams.

**Skills employers may look for**

Banking operations, financial services, process analysis, Excel, SQL, Power BI, workflow automation, AI fundamentals and communication.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Banking operations specialists, loan processing professionals, customer operations analysts and financial services professionals.

## **9. AI Insurance Analyst**

Insurance companies use data to assess claims, evaluate risks, estimate losses and support underwriting decisions.

AI Insurance Analysts work with AI-powered tools to analyze insurance data, identify patterns and improve operational processes.

For example, an insurance company may use computer vision to analyze photographs submitted with vehicle insurance claims. AI can help identify visible damage and organize information for further assessment.

An analyst may review the system\'s output, investigate exceptions and support claims or underwriting teams.

**What the role involves**

- Analyzing insurance claims and policy information.

- Supporting AI-assisted claims processing.

- Identifying unusual claims patterns.

- Reviewing risk assessment information.

- Monitoring the accuracy of automated workflows.

- Supporting reporting and operational improvements.

**Skills employers may look for**

Insurance fundamentals, claims processing, risk analysis, Excel, SQL, data analytics, AI fundamentals and process automation.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Insurance analysts, claims specialists, underwriters, actuarial support professionals and insurance operations specialists.

## **10. AI Finance Product Analyst**

Financial institutions are increasingly introducing digital products that incorporate AI, including financial planning applications, intelligent reporting platforms, fraud detection systems and AI-powered banking assistants.

An AI Finance Product Analyst works between business teams, product managers and technical teams to understand financial requirements and help develop AI-enabled products.

For example, a company developing an AI-powered personal finance application may need someone who understands budgeting, spending patterns and financial reporting to help define how the product should work.

The analyst translates financial use cases into product requirements and helps evaluate whether the solution meets customer and business needs.

**What the role involves**

- Identifying financial use cases for AI products.

- Gathering business and customer requirements.

- Working with product managers and engineering teams.

- Evaluating AI product functionality.

- Supporting product testing and user feedback.

- Measuring product performance and business impact.

**Skills employers may look for**

Financial services knowledge, business analysis, product fundamentals, requirements gathering, AI fundamentals, data analysis, communication and stakeholder management.

**Who can pursue this role?**

Business analysts, finance professionals, banking specialists, financial product analysts and professionals interested in moving into technology product roles.

### **Example: Build an AI Financial Statement Analyzer**

A practical project could allow users to upload a company\'s annual report and ask questions about its financial performance.

The application could:

1.  Extract financial information from uploaded reports.

2.  Identify revenue, expenses, assets and liabilities.

3.  Summarize year-over-year financial changes.

4.  Answer questions using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).

5.  Display relevant source references.

6.  Generate a structured financial summary for review.

This project can demonstrate knowledge of financial statements, document processing, LLM integration, RAG and application development.

It also helps finance professionals understand the difference between simply using an AI chatbot and building an AI-powered financial application.

Any such system should clearly identify its limitations and should not be treated as a substitute for audited financial statements or professional financial advice.

## **A Practical Roadmap: How to Transition from Finance to AI in the USA**

Finance professionals do not necessarily need to abandon their existing careers to enter the AI industry.

A structured learning approach can help them build on what they already know.

### **Step 1: Identify Your Existing Strengths**

Start by understanding your current experience.

An accountant may have strong knowledge of reconciliation and financial reporting. A banking professional may understand transactions, lending and fraud prevention. An investment analyst may already have experience with financial modeling and market research.

Identify which of these capabilities can be combined with AI.

### **Step 2: Learn AI and Generative AI Fundamentals**

Understand how generative AI, LLMs, prompting and AI-powered applications work.

Explore practical applications related to your existing finance responsibilities rather than learning tools without a clear purpose.

### **Step 3: Develop Data and Automation Skills**

Learn Excel, SQL, Power BI and workflow automation.

If you want to move into more technical positions, gradually introduce Python, APIs, databases and machine learning fundamentals.

### **Step 4: Build Finance-Focused AI Projects**

Develop projects that solve real problems in accounting, banking, financial analysis or compliance.

For example, create an invoice automation system, an AI financial reporting assistant or a financial document search application.

### **Step 5: Create a Relevant Portfolio**

Document your projects, including:

- The financial problem you wanted to solve.

- The technologies and AI tools you used.

- How the application works.

- What limitations you identified.

- How you evaluated the results.

### **Step 6: Target Relevant US Job Opportunities**

Search for positions using both traditional and emerging job titles.

Examples include:

- AI Financial Analyst.

- Finance Automation Analyst.

- Financial Data Analyst.

- AI Risk Analyst.

- Financial Crime Analytics Analyst.

- AI Governance Analyst.

- Banking Automation Specialist.

- AI Product Analyst -- Financial Services.

These are suggested search terms, not a claim that every employer uses these exact titles.

For international students and professionals in the USA, work authorization requirements should also be considered when evaluating job opportunities.

## **What Should International Finance Professionals in the USA Know?**

International students and working professionals in the United States may have additional considerations when transitioning into AI-related finance roles.

For F-1 students, employment options can include Curricular Practical Training (CPT) and Optional Practical Training (OPT). Eligible graduates in qualifying STEM programs may also apply for the 24-month STEM OPT extension.

However, eligibility depends on individual circumstances, the degree program, employment relationship and applicable immigration requirements.

Finance professionals on H-1B status should also understand that changing job responsibilities or employers may involve immigration and employment considerations. They should confirm their situation with a qualified immigration attorney.

Useful official resources:

- USCIS -- Optional Practical Training for F-1 Students.

- USCIS -- STEM OPT Extension.

- Study in the States -- Practical Training.

- USCIS -- H-1B Specialty Occupations.

This information is general guidance, not immigration advice.

## **What Does the Future of AI Careers in Finance Look Like?**

The finance industry is likely to continue integrating AI into its analytical, operational and decision-support processes.

Several developments are worth watching.

**1. AI-assisted financial analysis will become more common.**

Professionals may increasingly use AI to review financial documents, compare business performance and prepare initial reports. Human verification and financial judgment will remain important.

**2. Financial operations will become more automated.**

Invoice processing, reconciliation, expense management and reporting are areas where organizations can use automation to reduce repetitive work.

**3. AI governance and risk management will become increasingly important.**

Financial institutions need to understand how their AI systems make decisions, manage sensitive information and comply with applicable regulations.

**4. Finance professionals will need stronger data skills.**

Understanding Excel, SQL, data visualization and AI-powered analytical tools can help finance professionals work more effectively with technology teams.

**5. Domain expertise will remain valuable.**

AI systems do not automatically understand every financial situation. Professionals who combine finance knowledge with the ability to evaluate AI outputs can contribute to better business decisions.

The strongest opportunities may emerge for people who understand both the financial problem and the technology being used to solve it.

## **Frequently Asked Questions About AI Jobs for Finance Professionals**

### **1. Can finance professionals get jobs in AI?**

Yes. Finance professionals can explore AI-enabled roles in financial analysis, automation, risk management, fraud detection, compliance, investment research and financial product development. The required technical skills depend on the role.

### **2. Do I need Python to work in AI in finance?**

Not for every role. Professionals working in AI compliance, finance operations, product analysis and low-code automation may be able to start without extensive programming knowledge. Python becomes more useful for advanced analytics, machine learning and AI application development.

### **3. What are the best AI jobs for accountants?**

Accountants can explore AI Finance Automation Specialist, AI Financial Analyst, AI Compliance Analyst and AI FP&A Analyst roles. Their existing knowledge of financial reporting, reconciliation, auditing and accounting processes can provide a strong foundation.

### **4. Can banking professionals transition into AI careers?**

Yes. Banking professionals can build on their experience in transactions, lending, customer operations, risk management and compliance. Potential career directions include AI Fraud Detection Analyst, AI Risk Analyst and AI Banking Operations Analyst.

### **5. What is the salary of an AI Financial Analyst in the USA?**

There is no separate official BLS salary category for AI Financial Analysts. The broader Financial Analysts occupation had a median annual wage of \$103,570 in May 2025. Actual compensation for AI-enabled roles depends on responsibilities, experience, employer and location.

### **6. Is AI going to replace finance professionals?**

AI is likely to automate or change certain repetitive financial tasks, but financial judgment, regulatory interpretation, risk evaluation and business decision-making remain important. Professionals who learn to work with AI may be better positioned to adapt to changing responsibilities.

### **7. Can non-technical professionals work in AI in the finance industry?**

Yes. Some opportunities focus more on financial knowledge, business processes, compliance and product requirements than on software development. However, understanding AI capabilities, limitations and responsible use is increasingly useful.

### **8. What AI skills should finance professionals learn first?**

Start with generative AI fundamentals, prompt engineering, Excel, SQL and data visualization. Then explore workflow automation, APIs and Python depending on your career goals.

### **9. Are AI finance jobs available in the USA?**

Start with projects that address practical financial problems, such as an AI financial statement analyzer, invoice automation system, expense categorization tool or financial document assistant. Document the problem, solution, technologies, evaluation and limitations.
