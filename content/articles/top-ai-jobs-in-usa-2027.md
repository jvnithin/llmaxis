# **Top 10 AI Jobs in the USA in 2027: Salaries, Skills and Career Opportunities**

Artificial intelligence has moved well beyond the research lab. It is now being used to write software, detect financial fraud, support medical decisions, automate customer operations and help companies make sense of enormous amounts of data.

That shift is changing the kinds of people companies need.

A few years ago, most conversations about AI careers revolved around machine learning engineers and research scientists. Today, employers are also looking for people who can build applications around large language models, connect AI systems to company data, deploy models reliably and turn AI capabilities into products that solve business problems.

The numbers reflect this change. According to the 2026 Stanford AI Index, which uses US job-posting data from Lightcast, AI skills appeared in 2.5% of all US job postings in 2025, up 55% from the previous year. The same research found a particularly sharp rise in demand for skills related to agentic AI and AI agents.

But there is an important distinction: more companies mentioning AI in job descriptions does not mean every AI-related job is easy to get. Employers still expect candidates to demonstrate technical ability, practical experience and an understanding of how these systems work in real business environments.

So, which AI careers are worth paying attention to in the United States in 2027?

Here are ten roles to understand, what people in those roles actually do, the skills employers look for and what the available salary data tells us.

## **What the US AI job market is telling us**

Before looking at individual job titles, it helps to understand what is happening in the hiring market.

The 2026 Stanford AI Index, drawing on Lightcast job-posting data, highlights three developments:

- AI skills are appearing in a growing share of US job postings.

- Employers are asking for capabilities beyond basic chatbot usage.

- Skills associated with AI agents and agentic systems are gaining visibility in job descriptions.

One particularly interesting finding is the growth of agent-related skills.

The numbers do not prove that every company is building autonomous agents. They do show that employers are increasingly including these capabilities in their requirements.

For candidates, that is a useful signal: understanding how to build, connect and deploy AI applications is becoming more relevant than simply knowing how to use an AI chatbot.

## **1. AI / Machine Learning Engineer**

An AI or Machine Learning Engineer builds the systems that allow software to learn from data, make predictions and perform tasks that would otherwise require manual analysis.

In a typical role, you might work on a recommendation engine for an e-commerce company, a fraud detection model for a bank or a prediction system that helps a logistics company plan deliveries.

The job is not limited to training a model. Engineers also need to make sure it works with the company\'s existing software, handles real-world data and performs reliably after deployment.

**What the role involves**

- Developing and evaluating machine learning models.

- Preparing data for training and testing.

- Working with deep learning frameworks.

- Integrating models into applications and APIs.

- Monitoring model performance after deployment.

- Improving accuracy, speed and scalability.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Python, SQL, machine learning, PyTorch, TensorFlow, scikit-learn, statistics, data processing, model evaluation and cloud platforms.

**Where these professionals work**

Technology companies, financial institutions, healthcare organizations, retail businesses, automotive companies and enterprise software providers.

**Salary context**

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics does not publish a separate national wage figure for the exact title "AI Engineer" or "Machine Learning Engineer." Software developer and related occupational categories can provide context, but they are not exact equivalents. For a comparable official benchmark, the BLS reported a median annual wage of \$135,980 for software developers in May 2025.

This is a national occupational median, not a guaranteed salary for an AI/ML Engineer.

## **2. Generative AI Engineer**

Generative AI Engineers build applications that use models capable of producing text, images, code, audio and other content.

A practical example: imagine a healthcare company that wants employees to search internal policy documents and receive answers with references to the original material. A Generative AI Engineer could build that application using a large language model, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), a vector database and an API.

The role has become more visible as businesses move from experimenting with AI tools to integrating them into everyday operations.

Lightcast data published in the Stanford AI Index 2026 recorded 138,188 US AI job postings mentioning the skill "generative AI" in 2025, compared with 65,557 in 2024. That is a 111% increase in postings mentioning this skill.

**What the role involves**

- Building applications using large language models.

- Developing RAG-based systems.

- Connecting models to company databases and APIs.

- Designing prompts and managing model context.

- Evaluating output quality and reducing hallucinations.

- Managing latency, cost, security and reliability.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Python, LLM APIs, prompt engineering, RAG, embeddings, vector databases, LangChain, LlamaIndex, model evaluation, FastAPI and cloud deployment.

**Where these professionals work**

Enterprise software, healthcare technology, financial services, consulting, customer support platforms and AI product companies.

**Salary context**

"Generative AI Engineer" is an emerging job title and does not have a standalone BLS salary category. Compensation depends on the underlying engineering responsibilities, seniority, company and location. Compare actual job descriptions and salary ranges rather than relying on a single internet-wide average.

## **3. Data Scientist**

Data Scientists use statistics, programming and analytical methods to help organizations make decisions from data.

For example, a retail company may want to understand why customers stop buying its products. A Data Scientist could analyze customer behavior, identify patterns and build models to estimate which customers are most likely to leave.

AI tools can support this work, but the role still requires strong analytical thinking. Understanding the business question and interpreting the results correctly are just as important as writing code.

**What the role involves**

- Analyzing large datasets.

- Identifying trends and business patterns.

- Building predictive models.

- Designing experiments and evaluating outcomes.

- Communicating findings to business teams.

- Working with data engineers and product teams.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Python, SQL, statistics, probability, machine learning, data visualization, experimentation and tools such as Tableau or Power BI.

**Salary and employment outlook**

The BLS reported a median annual wage of \$120,230 for Data Scientists in May 2025. Its latest employment projections available for the 2025--2035 period show employment in this occupation growing by approximately 34.6%.

That projected growth is notable, although it is a forecast for the broader Data Scientist occupation---not a guarantee of individual job openings.

Data science can be a strong option for people who enjoy working with numbers, finding patterns and translating analysis into business decisions.

## **4. AI Research Scientist**

AI Research Scientists work on the development of new methods, models and techniques in artificial intelligence.

Their work may involve improving how language models reason, developing new machine learning architectures, exploring computer vision techniques or studying how AI systems can become more efficient and reliable.

This is generally a more research-intensive role than an application-focused AI engineering position.

A Research Scientist at an AI lab may spend months testing an idea, running experiments, reviewing academic literature and publishing findings. In an industrial research team, the work may also involve turning research results into technology that can be used in products.

**What the role involves**

- Conducting research in machine learning and AI.

- Designing experiments and evaluating hypotheses.

- Developing new algorithms and architectures.

- Reading and publishing research papers.

- Working with large datasets and computing infrastructure.

- Collaborating with engineers to test research outcomes.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Advanced mathematics, statistics, Python, PyTorch, TensorFlow, deep learning, optimization, research methodology and strong analytical skills.

**Education expectations**

Many research-focused positions prefer or require a master\'s degree or PhD, particularly when the work involves developing new algorithms or publishing original research. Requirements vary by employer and role.

**Salary and employment outlook**

The BLS reported a median annual wage of \$140,300 for Computer and Information Research Scientists in May 2025. Employment in this occupational category is projected to grow by approximately 21.8% from 2025 to 2035.

This is the closest broad BLS occupational category for discussing some AI research roles; it does not represent every position carrying the title "AI Research Scientist."

## **5. MLOps Engineer**

Building a machine learning model is only one part of the job. A company also needs to deploy it, monitor it, update it and make sure it continues to work as new data arrives.

That is where MLOps Engineers come in.

Think of a bank using a machine learning model to flag suspicious transactions. The model needs to run consistently, process incoming data, maintain acceptable response times and be monitored for performance changes. An MLOps Engineer helps build and maintain the infrastructure behind that system.

**What the role involves**

- Deploying machine learning models into production.

- Building automated training and deployment pipelines.

- Monitoring model performance and data quality.

- Managing model versions and experiments.

- Working with cloud infrastructure and containers.

- Improving system reliability and scalability.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Python, Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD, MLflow, cloud platforms, model monitoring, data pipelines and infrastructure automation.

**Why companies need this role**

A model that performs well in a notebook is not necessarily ready for production. Businesses need systems that are dependable, secure and maintainable. MLOps sits at the intersection of machine learning and software infrastructure.

## **6. Data Engineer**

AI systems depend on data. If that data is incomplete, outdated, inconsistent or poorly organized, even a sophisticated model can produce unreliable results.

Data Engineers build the systems that collect, process, transform and deliver data to the teams and applications that need it.

For example, an online retailer may collect information from its website, mobile app, payment platform and inventory system. Data Engineers create pipelines that bring these sources together so analysts and AI applications can use them.

**What the role involves**

- Building data pipelines and workflows.

- Designing and maintaining data warehouses.

- Processing structured and unstructured data.

- Improving data quality and accessibility.

- Managing large-scale data infrastructure.

- Supporting analytics and AI development teams.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

SQL, Python, Apache Spark, Airflow, dbt, Kafka, Snowflake, Databricks and cloud data services.

**Why it matters for AI careers**

Many AI projects fail for reasons that have little to do with the model itself. Data may be inaccessible, inconsistent or unsuitable for the intended application. Data Engineers help solve those problems before they become expensive production issues.

## **7. Natural Language Processing (NLP) Engineer**

Natural Language Processing Engineers develop systems that allow computers to understand, process and work with human language.

Examples include search engines, document classification systems, speech recognition, language translation, sentiment analysis and conversational applications.

The arrival of large language models has changed the field, but NLP remains relevant. Many enterprise applications still need language-specific processing, evaluation, search and information extraction.

**What the role involves**

- Developing text-processing systems.

- Working with language models and embeddings.

- Building information extraction and classification applications.

- Improving search and language understanding.

- Evaluating model performance across different types of text.

- Integrating language capabilities into software products.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Python, NLP fundamentals, transformers, Hugging Face, PyTorch, embeddings, tokenization, information retrieval and large language models.

**Where these professionals work**

Search technology, enterprise software, legal technology, healthcare, financial services and customer experience platforms.

## **8. AI Product Manager**

Not every AI career requires someone to train models or write complex algorithms.

AI Product Managers work between engineering teams, business stakeholders and customers. Their responsibility is to decide what an AI product should do, why it matters and how its success will be measured.

Consider a company planning to introduce an AI assistant for customer support. The AI Product Manager needs to understand customer problems, define the product requirements, work with engineers, assess risks and measure whether the assistant actually improves the customer experience.

**What the role involves**

- Identifying business problems that AI can address.

- Defining product requirements and priorities.

- Working with engineering, design and data teams.

- Evaluating AI product performance.

- Considering privacy, safety and responsible AI.

- Measuring adoption, customer satisfaction and business impact.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Product management, communication, analytical thinking, AI fundamentals, user research, product analytics, experimentation and stakeholder management.

**Who may be suited to this role?**

People with experience in product management, business analysis, consulting, operations or technology may find this role relevant. A deep machine learning background is not required for every AI Product Manager position, but understanding AI capabilities and limitations is essential.

## **9. AI Security Engineer**

As organizations introduce AI into business-critical systems, security teams need to consider risks that are specific to AI applications.

An AI Security Engineer works on protecting AI models, applications, data and infrastructure. This can include securing the systems that connect language models to internal databases, reviewing model access permissions and testing applications against attacks such as prompt injection.

For example, an enterprise AI assistant may have access to internal documents. If its permissions are poorly designed, a user could potentially retrieve information they should not be allowed to see. AI security teams help identify and reduce these risks.

**What the role involves**

- Assessing security risks in AI applications.

- Testing systems for prompt injection and data leakage.

- Securing model APIs and application infrastructure.

- Reviewing access controls and data permissions.

- Working with security and engineering teams.

- Supporting AI governance and risk management.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Cybersecurity, application security, cloud security, Python, API security, identity and access management, threat modeling and knowledge of LLM vulnerabilities.

**Salary context**

The BLS reported a median annual wage of \$129,180 for Information Security Analysts in May 2025. This is a broad cybersecurity benchmark, not a specific AI Security Engineer salary.

As AI security is still an evolving specialization, actual compensation depends heavily on the role\'s security responsibilities and the candidate\'s experience.

## **10. Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)**

Forward Deployed Engineers work closely with customers to solve complex technical and business problems using software and AI.

Unlike a traditional software engineer who may work primarily on a product from within an engineering team, an FDE often spends significant time understanding how a customer operates, identifying where technology can help and building solutions that work in that environment.

Imagine a large logistics company that wants to automate parts of its shipment planning process. An FDE might work with its operations team, connect internal data sources, build an AI-powered application and adapt the solution based on how employees actually use it.

The role has gained visibility as companies look for engineers who can move between software development, customer conversations and real-world implementation.

**What the role involves**

- Working directly with customers and business teams.

- Understanding operational and technical problems.

- Building and integrating software solutions.

- Connecting AI models to existing systems.

- Testing solutions in real business environments.

- Translating customer feedback into product improvements.

**Skills employers commonly look for**

Python, APIs, software engineering, LLM integration, RAG, databases, cloud platforms, debugging, communication and problem-solving.

**Salary context**

Forward Deployed Engineer is not a separately reported BLS occupation. Salary varies by employer, location, technical depth and customer-facing responsibilities. Candidates should compare the compensation ranges in individual employer listings rather than treating one online estimate as a national benchmark.

For people who enjoy both engineering and working directly with customers, this can be an interesting career direction.

## **Can you get an AI job in the USA without a computer science degree?**

Yes, but the answer depends on the role.

Some AI careers require a strong foundation in computer science, mathematics and software engineering. Others place greater emphasis on product knowledge, business experience, analytical ability or domain expertise.

For example:

- **AI/ML Engineer:** Usually requires strong programming, mathematics and machine learning knowledge.

- **Generative AI Engineer:** Requires software development skills and the ability to build applications around LLMs.

- **Data Scientist:** Often requires statistics, programming and analytical experience.

- **AI Product Manager:** May be accessible to professionals with product, business or technology experience.

- **FDE:** Requires strong engineering ability, communication and the confidence to work with customers.

- **AI Security Engineer:** Usually requires a cybersecurity or software security foundation.

A degree can help, especially for research-oriented positions. However, employers also evaluate whether candidates can demonstrate their ability to solve real problems.

A portfolio can help make that ability visible. For instance, a candidate interested in Generative AI could demonstrate an application that searches company documents, retrieves relevant information and generates answers with source references. A candidate interested in MLOps could show how a model is deployed, monitored and updated.

The project should demonstrate more than a working demo. It should explain the problem, technical decisions, limitations and results.

## **What should international students in the USA know about AI jobs?**

International students pursuing AI careers in the United States need to consider two separate questions:

1.  Are their skills relevant to the role?

2.  Is the employer able and willing to hire them under their current work authorization?

For F-1 students, practical training options generally include Curricular Practical Training (CPT) and Optional Practical Training (OPT). Eligible students in qualifying STEM degree programs may also be able to apply for the 24-month STEM OPT extension.

However, eligibility is not automatic. It depends on factors such as the degree program, the relationship between the work and the student\'s field of study, the employer\'s requirements and the student\'s immigration status.

Useful official resources:

- [[USCIS: Optional Practical Training (OPT) for F-1 Students]{.underline}](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students)

- [[USCIS: STEM OPT Extension]{.underline}](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/students-and-employment/stem-opt)

- [[Study in the States: Practical Training]{.underline}](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/students/work/working-in-the-united-states)

Students should confirm their individual eligibility with their Designated School Official (DSO) and, where appropriate, a qualified immigration attorney. This article is general information, not immigration advice.

## **What does the AI job market look like in 2027?**

No one can accurately predict every job title or hiring trend for 2027. But the latest available labor-market evidence points to several developments worth watching.

**1. AI application development is becoming more important.\**
Companies need engineers who can connect models to data, APIs and business software---not simply experiment with prompts.

**2. Agentic AI is appearing more often in job descriptions.\**
The increase in Agentic AI, AI Agents and LangGraph mentions in Lightcast data suggests growing employer interest in systems that can perform multi-step tasks.

**3. Data and infrastructure remain essential.\**
AI applications still depend on reliable data pipelines, cloud infrastructure, security and production monitoring. These needs support roles beyond model development itself.

**4. AI is becoming relevant outside traditional technology teams.\**
Product, operations, finance, healthcare and consulting teams are exploring AI applications. That creates opportunities for professionals who combine domain knowledge with an understanding of AI.

**5. Employers still need people who can deliver results.\**
Knowing the names of AI tools is not the same as being able to build a dependable solution. Candidates who can explain what they built, why they built it and how they evaluated it will have a stronger story to tell.

## **Frequently asked questions about AI jobs in the USA**

### **What is the highest-paying AI job in the USA?**

There is no single answer that applies to every employer. Senior AI research, machine learning, infrastructure and specialized engineering positions can command high compensation. Pay varies by experience, company, location and the responsibilities of the role. The BLS reported a May 2025 median annual wage of \$140,300 for Computer and Information Research Scientists, a broad category that includes some research-oriented AI work.

### **Are Generative AI Engineers in demand in the USA?**

Generative AI skills are appearing more frequently in US job postings. Lightcast data published in the Stanford AI Index 2026 recorded 138,188 US AI job postings mentioning the skill "generative AI" in 2025, compared with 65,557 in 2024. This measures skill mentions, not the number of jobs with the exact title "Generative AI Engineer."

### **Is AI a good career choice in 2027?**

AI can be a strong career direction for people who enjoy technology, problem-solving and continuous learning. However, the best opportunities are likely to go to candidates who can apply AI to real problems and demonstrate the value of their work. Choosing a role based on your existing strengths is more useful than pursuing a job title simply because it is popular.

### **Can non-IT professionals work in AI?**

Yes. AI Product Management, AI consulting, business analysis, AI operations and domain-specific AI roles may suit professionals from non-IT backgrounds. Technical positions such as AI/ML Engineering generally require deeper programming and mathematical skills.

### **Do AI jobs in the USA require a master\'s degree?**

Not all of them. Many engineering and product roles evaluate practical experience, technical ability and relevant education. Research-focused positions are more likely to require advanced degrees. Always check the requirements in individual job descriptions.

### **Which US states have the most AI job postings?**

In the 2025 Lightcast data published in the Stanford AI Index 2026, California had 170,881 AI job postings, followed by Texas with 80,547 and New York with 66,029.

### **Is Agentic AI a real career opportunity?**

Agentic AI is an emerging area of work rather than one standardized occupation. Employers may advertise related work under titles such as AI Engineer, Generative AI Engineer, Machine Learning Engineer or Software Engineer. The sharp increase in agent-related skill mentions in 2025 job postings suggests growing interest, but candidates should evaluate the actual responsibilities in each job description.

## **Final thoughts**

The US AI job market is expanding, but it is also becoming more specific about the problems companies want people to solve.

Some organizations need engineers who can train and deploy machine learning models. Others need people who can build LLM-powered applications, manage data infrastructure, secure AI systems or work directly with customers to implement new technology.

That is why choosing an AI career should start with understanding the work---not just the job title.

If you are exploring AI opportunities in the United States, pay attention to the skills appearing in real job descriptions, compare the responsibilities across roles and build evidence that you can solve practical problems. A well-explained project can tell an employer much more than a list of tools on a résumé.
